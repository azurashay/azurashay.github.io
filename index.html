01 / Problem

Sales weren't coming back

The platform captured rich engagement per account, but reps rarely came back to read it. We needed a view a rep could grasp in seconds, not skim in minutes.

02 / Insight

Engagement is a two-way story

What matters isn't a pile of views - it's the interplay between the rep's outreach and the lead's response. Both belong on the same timeline.

03 / Decision

One glanceable timeline

Per person, over time. Red dots for what the rep did, blue for how the lead responded, each sized by the strength of the touch. One glance shows who's warming up and where to push.

Case outcome ~15%

A reason to log back in

Reps got a picture of every account they could actually read - a reason to open the platform and act on it, instead of letting engagement data sit unread. Sales logins rose about 15%.

02 / Folloze Analytics Data viz

Folloze - Impact Dashboard

Board-level analytics that turn a running campaign into one readable story: who showed up, what they engaged with, and which channels brought them in. Marketers can prove impact without exporting a thing.

folloze · boards / virtual-events-awareness / impact
The campaign, in one screen. Five headline KPIs against the previous period, lead statuses, channel performance, top content, visits over time - the whole picture of a live campaign, on one page.
folloze · impact / engaged-accounts
Every number opens. Click a KPI and it drills into the accounts behind it - engagement scores, topics of interest, per-account trends the sales team can actually act on.
01 / Problem

Impact was invisible

Campaigns generated plenty of engagement. Proving it meant stitching numbers together from boards, accounts and channels by hand, after the fact - which almost nobody did.

02 / Insight

The story lives at the campaign level

People, accounts, content and sources only mean something together - one level above pageviews, one level below the quarterly report. That's where the story lives.

03 / Decision

Headlines first, drill-downs behind them

Five KPIs against the previous period carry the headline. Everything else - lead statuses, channels, content, trend - lives one click deeper, so the screen stays calm.

Case outcome 1 screen

The whole campaign, at a glance

One screen to see what a campaign actually did, and every number opens into the proof behind it. No exports, no stitching, no guesswork.

03 / Folloze AI Agent Conversational UI

Folloze Campaign Agent

A conversational AI that turns a few plain sentences about a product into a personalized B2B campaign - one question at a time, assembling everything a marketer needs to launch.

folloze · campaign-agent / build
The full flow. Describe a product, answer a few guided questions, and the agent builds a ready-to-launch board - Product, Audience, Message and Goal filling in as you go.
Designing the wait — generation takes real seconds, so the waiting experience is a designed surface, not a spinner.
Building your page. A four-stage sequence - thinking, diagnostics, generating structure, designing - plays while the agent assembles the board.
Thinking. A lightweight inline indicator for the shorter waits between steps.
01 / Problem

The blank-canvas tax

Starting an ABM campaign meant a dozen decisions at once - product, audience, message, goal. Heavy up front, slow to move, easy to abandon.

02 / Insight

Talking beats form-filling

People describe what they sell in plain words a lot more easily than they fill out forms. So let the conversation do the structuring.

03 / Decision

An agent that asks, then assembles

A chat that asks one question at a time, with a live panel mirroring the progress - Product, Audience, Message, Goal - ending in a ready-to-launch board.

Case outcome ~28%

From idea to board in minutes

Trading the blank canvas for a guided conversation lifted campaign-page creation by roughly 28%, and cut the time to prepare a page from hours to minutes.

Under the hood · prompt engineering

The simple chat hides two layers of prompt engineering: one that gives every building block meaning, and one that assembles them into a page.

01 · Section prompts

Every block knows itself

Each section carries its own content and its own purpose, so the agent knows what a block contains and what it's there to do.

02 · Page-builder agent

Assembles the page

A second prompt takes everything the user gave - product, audience, message, goal - and decides which sections to pull, and how to compose them into a finished page.

Color usage — when to reach for what

Deep navy · Primary actions

The confident default — main buttons, key CTAs, headings. If it drives the flow forward, it's navy.

Purple · Brand accent

Highlights, active states, links, selected tabs. Sparingly used, so it still means something.

Coral · Emphasis

Kept for warm, standout moments - promotions, celebrations, small delights. Rare on purpose.

Neutrals · Text & structure

Body copy, borders, surfaces, dividers - roughly 90% of every screen. The quiet backbone that everything else sits on.

Success / Info · Feedback

Confirmed states and helpful context. It always means something - never decoration.

Warning / Error · Attention

Something needs a second look or a fix. Used only when the user really has to notice.

04 / Dell EMC Enterprise SaaS Information architecture

Dell EMC XtremIO - Dashboard

A storage-management dashboard that showed every metric to every user with equal weight. I turned a wall of data into three focused views, one per audience.

xtremio · dashboard / performance
Performance view. IOPS, latency, bandwidth and capacity at a glance - built for the operators who live here every day.
xtremio · data-protection / overview
Data Protection view. RPO compliance, protection sessions and the replication topology - the backup team's priorities on a screen of their own.
01 / Problem

One screen, everyone, everything

The original dashboard threw health, performance, capacity, top volumes and weekly patterns at every user with equal priority. Heavy on cognitive load, thin on hierarchy.

02 / Insight

It wasn't one audience - it was three

Session recordings and work with the PM surfaced three distinct groups. Each one cared about a single metric first, and treated the rest as context.

03 / Decision

Three tabs, three priorities

I split the dashboard into three focused tabs, each owning one group's key metric - with a summary strip up top, so no one felt they were missing the wider picture.

Case outcome 3 ×

Calmer, more confident usage

Users settled into the tab built for them and navigated outward from there - exactly the behaviour the structure was designed to create. Reported experience improved noticeably, and the "wall of data" complaints stopped.
